# FAQ

Answers to the questions people most often search for about the Caliper test. The questions come from Google's "People also ask" and "Things to know" results; the answers come from Caliper's and Talogy's own pages, listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## What is a Caliper assessment?

A workplace personality and reasoning assessment, now sold by Talogy as Talogy Caliper and formerly known as the Caliper Profile. Caliper says it measures personality characteristics and motivations to predict on-the-job behaviour and potential. It scores 21 personality traits plus Abstract Reasoning. Full details in [the Caliper Profile explained](test-overview.md).

## What is the purpose of the Caliper test?

Hiring first, and then development. Caliper says the same results can be used across the employee lifecycle: selection, development, promotion, team building and succession planning. For hiring, the results are compared with a job model to produce a fit score.

## How long does the Caliper test take?

It is untimed. Caliper's FAQ says it takes about 60 minutes; Talogy's support article says most people finish in 60 to 75 minutes. A shortened version takes about 25 minutes and leaves out the reasoning section.

## What types of questions are on the Caliper test?

Three types, according to Talogy: forced-choice sets, where you pick the statement most like you and the one least like you (the bulk of the test); Likert items, where you rate your agreement from 1 to 7; and abstract reasoning items such as number patterns and shape analogies. There are 98 questions, not counting experimental ones. Each type has a guide in this repo: [forced choice](sections/forced-choice.md), [Likert](sections/likert.md) and [abstract reasoning](sections/abstract-reasoning.md).

## How is the Caliper test scored?

Each trait is scored as a percentile against people in your country of employment, where 50 is average. Traits are combined into behaviours, competencies and a job-fit score from 1 to 99 for the job model the employer chose. Talogy reads 60 to 79 as good alignment and 80 to 99 as strong alignment. See [How scoring works](scoring.md).

## Can you fail the Caliper test?

Not in the usual sense. There is no pass mark, and only the abstract reasoning items have right answers. The result is a fit score for a particular job model, and Caliper's own sample report says results should never be the only basis for a hiring decision.

## How do I read Caliper test results?

Start with the job-fit score and Talogy's bands, then the competencies, then the traits. Talogy's main tip is never to interpret a single trait on its own, because traits combine to shape behaviour. The [traits guide](traits.md) explains what each one means.

## Can I see my Caliper results?

Not from Caliper. Caliper says it does not send results to the people who take the profile. The client company owns the results and decides whether to share them, so ask the employer.

## How do I prepare for the Caliper test?

Learn the three formats, settle on an honest frame for answering ("me at work, on a typical week"), and practise the reasoning items, which are the only ones with right answers. Then plan a quiet, uninterrupted hour or so. The [7-day and 2-day plans](study-plans.md) set this out step by step.

## How do I pass a personality test for a job?

By answering honestly and consistently. Personality tests like the Caliper Profile have no right answers; they describe how you tend to work. Talogy says the forced-choice format, the length of the assessment and local norms make deliberate manipulation hard. A profile that matches you is also the one that leads to a job you will do well in. Read [why faking backfires](sections/forced-choice.md#why-faking-backfires).

## Can you fake the Caliper test?

Talogy says the profile is designed to resist deliberate manipulation, and that it is updated regularly partly to reduce fakability. The forced-choice format stops you rating every flattering statement as top, and keeping up a false picture over 98 questions is hard. Trying usually produces a muddled profile rather than an impressive one.

## Is the Caliper test hard?

The personality sections are not hard, but they are long. The reading level is US grade 6 to 8, according to Talogy. The abstract reasoning items are the challenging part, because they are the only ones with right answers. Practice with the [reasoning guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md) helps.

## Is there a time limit on each section?

No. Caliper says the profile is untimed. Your progress is saved at the last section you completed, so a dropped connection does not lose your work.

## What happens if my connection drops?

Caliper says the assessment resumes after the last section you completed. If the link will not open, contact the company that sent it.

## Is the Caliper Profile available in other languages?

Yes. Caliper's FAQ lists 27 languages and variants, including UK and US English, Canadian French, Mexican and European Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Arabic, Hebrew, Japanese, Korean, Thai and both forms of Chinese. Ask the employer which ones they can send.

## Is the Caliper test legal and fair?

Caliper says the profile was developed in line with US Department of Labor best practice and professional guidelines for selection tests, meets EEOC requirements, and is monitored each year for adverse impact and validity.

## Where can I practise for free?

The [worked examples and practice items in this repo](sections/forced-choice.md#practice-items), and the [free Caliper Profile practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/caliper-profile?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=56kub3&utm_content=caliper-profile), which gives feedback on every answer.
