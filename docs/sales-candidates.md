# For sales candidates

If you have been sent a Caliper Profile for a sales role, you are taking the assessment in the setting it was first built for. This page covers what that means for you.

## Built for sales

Caliper says Herb Greenberg, PhD, built the Caliper Profile almost 60 years ago to predict the sales success of insurance sales candidates. Caliper was founded in 1961, and Greenberg's 1963 Harvard Business Review article, "What Makes a Good Salesman", was one of its early milestones. A second article, "Job Matching for Better Sales Performance", followed in 1980.

Today the profile is used for every kind of role, but sales is still a large part of its use. Caliper's research on mental toughness, which it first studied in professional sports, also focuses on sales. Caliper names six traits behind it: Level-Headedness, Stress Tolerance, Ego-Strength (resilience), Energy (persistence), Self-Structure and Thoroughness.

## The sales job models

Caliper lists 11 validated job models for sales professions:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Account Development | Account Service Specialist | Agency Recruiter |
| Consultative Selling | Corporate Recruiter | Hunter |
| New Business Development | Producer | Sales Manager |
| Strategic Selling | Technical Sales | |

Each job model is a set of 5 to 9 competencies that Talogy says are key to success in that type of job. Your fit score is calculated against the model your employer picked. See [How scoring works](scoring.md#the-job-fit-score).

## What this means for you

**Different sales jobs need different people.** A hunter role built around cold outreach and a consultative role built around long relationships are separate job models with different competencies. There is no single "sales personality" the profile is looking for. Knowing which kind of sales role you have applied for helps you understand the fit score if it is shared with you. It should not change how you answer.

**The temptation to "sound like a salesperson" is strongest here.** Statements about persuading people, shrugging off rejection or chasing targets can look like the right picks for a sales job. They are only right if they are true of you. Talogy says the forced-choice format, the length of the assessment and local norms make deliberate manipulation hard. And if it did work, it would match you to a sales job that suits someone else, which is a poor trade for you as well as for the employer.

**Abstract Reasoning still counts.** Sales roles involve understanding products, pricing and client problems. The reasoning section is the only part with right answers, and it is the part where practice helps most. Work through the [abstract reasoning guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md).

## Questions to ask the employer

These are reasonable questions to ask before or after you take the profile:

- Which part of the hiring process does the Caliper Profile feed into?
- Is it the full profile or a shortened version?
- Will I get any feedback on my results? (Caliper does not send results to candidates; the employer decides.)

## Practise before you take it

Get familiar with the formats in the [forced-choice](sections/forced-choice.md) and [Likert](sections/likert.md) guides, then try a longer run with feedback on the [free Caliper Profile practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/caliper-profile?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=56kub3&utm_content=caliper-profile).
