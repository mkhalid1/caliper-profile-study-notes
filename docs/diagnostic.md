# 15-minute diagnostic

Nine original items, three for each of the Caliper Profile's question types. The personality items have no right answers on the real test, so here they check something different: whether you understand how the format works and can answer consistently. The reasoning items have real answers.

These items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Caliper Profile or from any Caliper or Talogy material.

## How to take it

1. Answer all nine without looking anything up. There is no time limit, but most people need about 8 minutes.
2. Check your answers and read every explanation. That takes another 5 to 7 minutes.
3. Use the table at the bottom to choose where to start.

If you already know how the formats work, the [free Caliper Profile practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/caliper-profile?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=caliper-profile) gives you a longer run with feedback on every answer.

## Part A: Forced choice

**D1.** Using only what the description says, which statement is **most** like Tom, and which is **least** like him?

> Tom calls clients back the same day, every time. He hates leaving a task half done and always finishes one job before starting the next. He says untested ideas make him nervous and he would rather stick with what has worked.

A) I finish what I start before moving on.
B) I like taking a chance on an idea nobody has tried.
C) I prefer working in a quiet room.
D) I enjoy a lively debate.

**D2.** According to Talogy, which question type makes up most of the Caliper Profile?
A) Forced choice  B) Likert scales  C) Abstract reasoning  D) Written answers

**D3.** Why does a "most like me and least like me" format make it hard to paint a falsely perfect picture?
A) You cannot mark every attractive statement as most like you; you have to choose between them.
B) Each set has a strict time limit.
C) A proctor checks each answer.
D) Each set has one correct answer.

## Part B: Likert items

In this repo's Likert items, 1 means strongly disagree, 4 means neither agree nor disagree, and 7 means strongly agree. Always read the labels on your own screen.

**D4.** Ravi loves meeting new people at conferences and is usually the one who starts the conversation. How should he rate this statement? *"I feel comfortable starting conversations with people I don't know."*
A) 1 or 2  B) 3, 4 or 5  C) 6 or 7

**D5.** Earlier in the assessment, Lena honestly rated *"I enjoy work that follows a steady routine"* as a 7. Later she sees *"Repetitive tasks quickly bore me."* Answering honestly and consistently, which rating fits?
A) 1 or 2  B) 4  C) 6 or 7

**D6.** On a 1 to 7 agreement scale, what does a 4 mean?
A) Strongly agree  B) Neither agree nor disagree  C) Strongly disagree  D) I did not read the statement

## Part C: Abstract reasoning

**D7.** What comes next? 6, 11, 16, 21, ?
A) 24  B) 25  C) 26  D) 27

**D8.** What comes next? 1, 4, 16, 64, ?
A) 128  B) 192  C) 256  D) 320

**D9.** ◇ is to ◆ as □ is to:
A) □  B) ■  C) ◇  D) ●

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

**D1: Most A, least B.** Tom always finishes one job before the next, which is statement A. He is nervous about untested ideas, which is the opposite of B. The description says nothing about quiet rooms or debates, so C and D are neither most nor least.

**D2: A) Forced choice.** Talogy says the bulk of the questions are forced choice, with Likert and abstract reasoning items as well.

**D3: A.** In a forced-choice set you cannot rate every flattering statement as top; you must pick one as most like you and one as least. Talogy says research shows this format reduces fakability. *Why not B and C:* the profile is untimed and Caliper says no proctor is required. *Why not D:* only the abstract reasoning items have right answers.

**D4: C) 6 or 7.** The statement describes exactly what Ravi says he does and enjoys.

**D5: A) 1 or 2.** "Repetitive tasks bore me" is the reverse of "I enjoy a steady routine". If she strongly agrees with the first, the honest answer to the second is to disagree.

**D6: B) Neither agree nor disagree.** On a 1 to 7 scale, 4 is the middle point. Use it when you are genuinely mixed, not as a safe default.

**D7: C) 26.** The step is +5 each time: 21 + 5 = 26.

**D8: C) 256.** Each number is four times the one before: 64 × 4 = 256.

**D9: B) ■.** The hollow diamond becomes a filled diamond, so the hollow square becomes a filled square. The shape stays the same; only the fill changes.

</details>

## What your result means

Count your correct answers in each part.

| Part | Your score | If you scored 0 or 1 | If you scored 2 | If you scored 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Forced choice | /3 | Start with the [forced-choice guide](sections/forced-choice.md) | Read its method and do the worked examples | Light review only |
| B. Likert | /3 | Start with the [Likert guide](sections/likert.md) | Practise the consistency check | Light review only |
| C. Abstract reasoning | /3 | Start with the [abstract reasoning guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md) | Learn the pattern checklist | Light review only |

Whatever your score, read the [traits guide](traits.md) once. Knowing what the report describes makes the personality sections much less mysterious.

Then pick a plan: the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) if you have a week, or the [2-day plan](study-plans.md#2-day-plan) if the link has already arrived.
