# How Caliper Profile scoring works

Short version: your answers produce a percentile score for each trait, compared with people in the country where you will work. Caliper combines the traits into behaviours and competencies, and finally a job-fit score from 1 to 99 for the job model your employer picked. There is no pass mark, and the results go to the employer, not to you.

Everything below comes from Talogy's and Caliper's own pages, linked at the end.

## Trait scores are percentiles

Talogy's scoring guide says each trait is scored as a percentile showing a person's degree of motivation relative to others in their country of employment. A score of 50 means an average degree of that motivation.

Three things Talogy stresses:

- **Nobody scores high on everything,** and nobody should expect to. A typical profile is a mix of highs and lows.
- **High is not good and low is not bad.** Each trait can be a strength or a drawback depending on the job. Low Urgency, for instance, can suit work that rewards taking time.
- **Never read one trait alone.** Traits combine to shape behaviour, so the pattern matters more than any single number.

Abstract Reasoning is scored as a percentile too, but it is the one trait measured with right and wrong answers.

## Norm groups

Talogy says Caliper uses norm groups, samples of test takers representative of the population the assessment is meant for, to turn raw responses into "culturally relevant percentile scores." Caliper's FAQ adds that scoring is based on the culture or country in which the person will work. The sample report published on Caliper's site, for example, is labelled with US scoring norms.

In practice: you are compared with people in your working country, not with a global average or an ideal candidate.

## Behaviours, competencies and job models

| Layer | How Talogy describes it |
|---|---|
| Behaviours | Predictions of visible actions, each driven by a validated combination of traits |
| Competencies | Predictions of performance in a role's critical outcome areas, built from trait scores |
| Job models | A set of 5 to 9 competencies key to success in a type of job |

Caliper's site lists 280 behaviours and 56 competencies. Its Essentials for Selection report gives an overall fit score for 52 validated job models, including 11 for sales. If none fits a role, Talogy says an employer can ask a Caliper consultant for a customised recommendation.

## The job-fit score

Talogy's job-fit score predicts how likely someone is to succeed in a particular job model, on a scale from 1 to 99. Its interpretation bands:

| Score | Talogy's interpretation, in short |
|---|---|
| 1 to 39 | Limited alignment; likely to meet challenges in the role |
| 40 to 59 | Moderate alignment; some strengths, some possible challenges |
| 60 to 79 | Good alignment; likely to succeed with support in development areas |
| 80 to 99 | Strong alignment; high potential for success |

The same profile gives different fit scores for different job models. That is why Talogy says choosing the right job model for the role is critical, and why one person can be a strong fit for one job and a moderate fit for another.

## What the employer's report shows

Caliper's Essentials for Selection report includes:

- an overall fit score for the chosen job model
- scores for the role's critical, important and supporting competencies
- key findings and suggested behavioural interview questions
- a summary of strengths and development areas
- how the person communicates, works with others, solves problems, makes decisions and organises time
- scores for all the traits

A shorter Highlights report gives recruiters the competency scores without the interview questions or recommendations. Caliper's sample report reminds employers that the results should never be the only basis for a hiring, development or promotion decision, and should be read alongside interviews, the CV and references.

## Will I see my results?

Not from Caliper. Caliper's FAQ says it does not send results to the people who take the profile. The results, including the percentile data, become the property of the client company, which decides whether to share them. If you want feedback, ask the employer. Some use Caliper's debrief consultations, which Talogy offers as an add-on.

## What counts as a good result

There is no pass mark. The job-fit score depends on the job model, and the trait scores are not good or bad on their own. Talogy's bands above are the only published guide to reading a fit score.

What you control is accuracy. An honest, consistent profile gives a fit score that reflects how well the job is likely to suit you. A distorted one can put you in a role that suits someone else. See [why faking backfires](sections/forced-choice.md#why-faking-backfires).
