# The Caliper Profile explained

**In one line:** an untimed, hour-long workplace assessment of 98 questions that measures 21 personality traits and one reasoning ability, then compares you with the profile of people who succeed in the job.

The Caliper Profile is a pre-employment and development assessment, now sold by Talogy as Talogy Caliper. Employers use it to hire, and again later to coach, promote and plan succession. Everything on this page comes from Caliper's and Talogy's own pages, listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Talogy (Caliper was founded in 1961 by Herb Greenberg, PhD) |
| Questions | 98, not counting experimental items |
| Time | Untimed. Caliper says about 60 minutes; Talogy says most people finish in 60 to 75. |
| Question types | Forced choice (most of the test), Likert agreement scales, abstract reasoning |
| What it measures | 21 personality traits plus Abstract Reasoning |
| Right answers? | Only in the abstract reasoning items. The personality items have none. |
| Reading level | US grade 6 to 8, according to Talogy |
| Results | Trait percentiles and a job-fit score from 1 to 99. They go to the employer, not to you. |
| Short version | About 25 minutes, without the abstract reasoning section, for some entry-level roles |

## What it measures

Caliper describes the profile as an assessment of personality characteristics and motivations, used to predict on-the-job behaviour and potential. Talogy's scoring guide is careful about one point: apart from Abstract Reasoning, the traits are **motivations or natural tendencies, not abilities**. A high or low score on a trait is not good or bad in itself. It can be a strength in one job and a drawback in another.

The traits are combined into predicted behaviours, then competencies, then a fit score for a job model. The [traits guide](traits.md) lists all of them and explains how that works.

## The three question types

| Type | What you do | Right answer? | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forced choice | From a set of statements, pick the one most like you and the one least like you | No | [Forced choice](sections/forced-choice.md) |
| Likert | Rate how much you agree with a statement on a scale of 1 to 7 | No | [Likert items](sections/likert.md) |
| Abstract reasoning | Solve number patterns and shape analogies | Yes | [Abstract reasoning](sections/abstract-reasoning.md) |

Talogy says the bulk of the questions are forced choice, and that abstract reasoning items are the only ones with correct and incorrect answers.

## How the test is built

- **Untimed, but long.** There is no clock, but an hour of honest self-description is tiring. Plan a quiet block of time.
- **Forced choice to resist faking.** Talogy says the forced-choice format, the length of the assessment and local norms all make deliberate manipulation hard. More in [why faking backfires](sections/forced-choice.md#why-faking-backfires).
- **Experimental questions.** Talogy says the profile includes sets of newly developed questions that are being tested before they become part of the assessment, and that the profile is updated regularly.
- **Saved as you go.** Caliper says your progress is saved at the last section you completed, so a dropped connection does not wipe your work.
- **Online and mobile friendly.** Caliper says no proctor is required, although an employer can ask to proctor it in its own office.

## Who uses it and when

Caliper says the profile is used across the employee lifecycle: selection, development, promotion, team building and succession planning. As a candidate you usually meet it after an application or first interview, as an emailed link. The employer can set the link to expire after a set number of days, so check the email for a deadline.

Caliper was first built to predict sales success, and many sales employers still use it. If that is you, see the page [for sales candidates](sales-candidates.md).

## The short version

Caliper offers a shortened assessment of about 25 minutes, which Talogy calls the Caliper Predictor Plus. It leaves out the abstract reasoning section, and Caliper recommends it only for entry-level roles. If an employer later wants the full profile for someone who took the short version, Caliper says they only need to take the cognitive portion.

## Languages

Caliper's FAQ lists these languages: Arabic, Bahasa Indonesian, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Czech, Danish, Dutch, UK English, US English, Finnish, French, Canadian French, German, Hebrew, Hungarian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Malay, Norwegian, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Mexican Spanish, European Spanish, Swedish and Thai. Caliper's own pages give different totals elsewhere, so ask the employer which languages they can send.

## Practise the format

The personality sections reward honest, consistent answers, not preparation. What practice does help with is familiarity: knowing how a "most and least" set feels, what the scale means, and what kinds of reasoning puzzles appear. Take the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md), or try the [free Caliper Profile practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/caliper-profile?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=56kub3&utm_content=caliper-profile), which gives feedback on every answer.
