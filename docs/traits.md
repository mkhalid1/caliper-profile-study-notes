# The Caliper traits

**In one line:** your report scores 21 personality traits and Abstract Reasoning; Caliper combines them into behaviours, competencies and a fit score for a job model.

You do not need to know the traits to take the Caliper Profile, and trying to steer your answers towards them backfires (see [why faking backfires](sections/forced-choice.md#why-faking-backfires)). But knowing what the report describes takes the mystery out of an hour of personality questions, and it helps you talk about your results if an employer shares them.

## How Caliper counts the traits

Caliper and Talogy describe the profile as measuring 21 personality traits and one cognitive ability, Abstract Reasoning. Talogy's trait glossary lists all 22 together, and its scoring guide says, "With the exception of Abstract Reasoning, traits should be thought of as motivations or natural tendencies rather than abilities." Talogy adds that a typical person has a mix of high and low trait scores, that nobody scores high on everything, and that each trait can be a strength or a drawback depending on the job.

## All 22 traits

The definitions below are in our own words, based on Talogy's Glossary of Caliper Traits. The grouping is ours, to make the list easier to read. It is not an official Caliper grouping.

### Working with people

| Trait | A high score suggests | A low score suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Accommodation | A strong wish to help others | Less interest in helping out |
| Empathy | Reading people's feelings and social cues well | Missing or not noticing others' feelings |
| Gregariousness | Ease meeting new people and starting conversations | Unease taking the first step socially |
| Sociability | Enjoying being around people and working with them | Less interest in frequent social contact |
| Skepticism | Questioning others' motives; guarded | Trusting; giving the benefit of the doubt |

### Influencing and drive

| Trait | A high score suggests | A low score suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Assertiveness | Stating ideas and opinions directly | Discomfort voicing a view |
| Aggressiveness | Pushing forcefully for your ideas or actions | Rarely taking a firm approach |
| Ego-Drive | Satisfaction from persuading people and winning commitment | Little effort spent on winning people round |
| Ego-Strength | Handling rejection and criticism without being thrown | Sensitivity to criticism or rejection |
| Energy | Keeping up a high level of activity for long periods | A less energetic approach to work |
| Urgency | Acting fast to get immediate results | Taking time over tasks |

### Steadiness and pressure

| Trait | A high score suggests | A low score suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Level-Headedness | Staying calm and steady day to day | Responding more emotionally |
| Stress Tolerance | Staying unworried about things beyond your control | Focusing on what might go wrong |

### Structure and approach to work

| Trait | A high score suggests | A low score suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Cautiousness | Deciding carefully after weighing facts and options | Acting without thinking things through |
| External Structure | Comfort with rules and a structured setting | Less responsiveness to authority |
| Self-Structure | Wanting to set your own work methods | Less inclination to define your own approach |
| Thoroughness | Close attention to detail | Less interest in fine points |
| Flexibility | Adapting your approach as things change | Reluctance to change approach |
| Openness | Being open to other people's ideas | Focusing on your own way of doing things |
| Idea Orientation | Generating creative, original solutions | Preferring well-established methods |
| Risk-Taking | Taking chances on untested initiatives | Reluctance to risk failure |

### Reasoning

| Trait | A high score suggests | A low score suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract Reasoning | Understanding complex issues and logical relationships | Doing best with problems that have straightforward solutions |

Abstract Reasoning is the only one measured with right and wrong answers. See the [abstract reasoning guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md).

## From traits to job fit

Talogy's scoring guide describes four layers, each built on the one before:

| Layer | What it is | Talogy's example |
|---|---|---|
| Traits | Hidden motivations and tendencies, each scored as a percentile | Flexibility, Openness, Abstract Reasoning |
| Behaviours | Visible actions predicted from a validated combination of traits | "Demonstrates an openness to learning new things" |
| Competencies | Outcome areas built from several behaviours over time | Learning Agility |
| Job models | A set of 5 to 9 key competencies for a type of job | A fit score from 1 to 99 |

Talogy says each trait percentile compares you with others in your country of employment, and that a score of 50 indicates an average degree of motivation.

Caliper's site lists 280 behaviours and 56 competencies, and its selection report gives fit scores for 52 validated job models, 11 of them for sales roles.

Talogy's key tip for anyone reading a report is the one to remember: **never interpret a single trait on its own.** Traits always combine to shape behaviour. More on reading the numbers in [How scoring works](scoring.md).

## Practice items

These 5 items check that you can read the traits the way a report describes them. They are original, written for these notes, and based only on the definitions above.

For items 1 to 3, choose the trait that best fits the description.

**Item 1.** When a customer turns Raj down, he is not thrown by it and is ready for the next one.
A) Ego-Drive  B) Ego-Strength  C) Empathy  D) Energy

**Item 2.** Ben wants to decide for himself how he will do his work.
A) External Structure  B) Self-Structure  C) Flexibility  D) Openness

**Item 3.** Dana is driven to act quickly to get results straight away.
A) Energy  B) Urgency  C) Risk-Taking  D) Thoroughness

**Item 4.** According to Talogy, what does a trait score of 50 mean?
A) You answered half the questions correctly.
B) An average degree of that motivation compared with others in your country of employment.
C) You failed that trait.
D) The trait could not be measured.

**Item 5.** Which trait is measured with items that have right and wrong answers?
A) Thoroughness  B) Level-Headedness  C) Abstract Reasoning  D) Idea Orientation

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: B) Ego-Strength.** Handling rejection and criticism without being thrown. *Why not A:* Ego-Drive is about enjoying persuasion, not about bouncing back.

**Item 2: B) Self-Structure.** Preferring to set your own work methods. *Why not A:* External Structure is about comfort with existing rules, which is close to the opposite.

**Item 3: B) Urgency.** The drive to act quickly for immediate results. *Why not A:* Energy is about sustaining activity over long periods, not speed to results.

**Item 4: B.** Talogy says each trait is scored as a percentile against others in your country of employment, and 50 indicates an average degree of motivation. Personality traits are not pass or fail.

**Item 5: C) Abstract Reasoning.** Talogy says abstract reasoning items are the only ones with correct and incorrect answers.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | B | B | B | B | C |

- **5:** you can read a Caliper report. If an employer shares yours, you will be able to discuss it with confidence.
- **3 to 4:** the pairs that trip people up are Ego-Drive and Ego-Strength, Self-Structure and External Structure, and Urgency and Energy. Reread those rows.
- **2 or fewer:** read the tables once more, then redo the items.

Knowing the traits is for understanding your report, not for steering your answers. The personality items still have no right answers.
