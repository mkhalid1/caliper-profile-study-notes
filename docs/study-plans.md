# Study plans

Two plans: one for a week, one for when the link has already arrived. Preparing for the Caliper Profile is different from preparing for a pure aptitude test. Most of it has no right answers, so the aim is to understand the formats, settle on an honest frame for answering, and sharpen up for the one section that is scored right or wrong.

Both plans start with the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md).

## 7-day plan

About 20 to 40 minutes a day.

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Read [the Caliper Profile explained](test-overview.md). Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | Forced choice | Read the [forced-choice guide](sections/forced-choice.md) and do its worked examples. Write one sentence describing yourself at work on a typical week. That sentence is your frame for the whole test. |
| 3 | Likert items | Read the [Likert guide](sections/likert.md) and do its examples. Note which trap caught you: negatives, the middle point or drifting frames. |
| 4 | Abstract reasoning | Read the [abstract reasoning guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md). Do the 12 items, then learn the number-pattern checklist. |
| 5 | The traits and the report | Read the [traits guide](traits.md) and [How scoring works](scoring.md). Do the trait items. This is for understanding, not for steering your answers. |
| 6 | Full practice run | Take a longer practice run, such as the [free Caliper Profile practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/caliper-profile?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=caliper-profile), in one sitting. Notice when you start to tire and your answers get careless. |
| 7 | Rest and plan the session | Redo any reasoning items you missed. Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md) and book a quiet 75-minute slot. |

### How to review

For the **reasoning items**, ask three questions about each miss:

1. Which rule did I miss, or which second change did I overlook?
2. What in the item should have tipped me off?
3. What will I check next time?

For the **personality items**, there is nothing to "review" in the usual sense. Instead, check consistency: did you answer from the same frame throughout? Did you rate any statement for how it sounds rather than how true it is? If so, that is the habit to drop.

## 2-day plan

For when the link has arrived and the deadline is close.

### Day 1: learn the formats

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 min | Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | 20 min | Read "How the questions look", the method and the traps in the [forced-choice](sections/forced-choice.md) and [Likert](sections/likert.md) guides. |
| 3 | 25 min | Read the [abstract reasoning guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md) and do its 12 items. |
| 4 | 10 min | Skim the [traits guide](traits.md) so the report language is familiar. |

### Day 2: take the test

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 min | Redo any reasoning items you missed yesterday. |
| 2 | 5 min | Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). Write your one-sentence frame: you at work on a typical week. |
| 3 | 75 min | Take the Caliper Profile in a quiet room, at a time of day when you are fresh. |

If you only have one evening, do blocks 1 and 3 of day 1, read the checklist, and take the test the next morning.

## What not to do

- **Do not hunt for "ideal answers" for a job.** Talogy says the format is designed to resist manipulation, and it updates the profile regularly. A faked profile, if it worked, would only match you to a job that suits someone else.
- **Do not memorise the trait list to steer your answers.** Traits are built from patterns across many answers, and you cannot track that while answering honestly. Read the traits to understand the report, then forget them.
- **Do not split the test into many short sessions if you can avoid it.** Progress saves at each completed section, which helps if your connection drops, but one steady sitting keeps your frame consistent.
- **Do not leave it until the link expires.** Employers can set the link to stop working after a set number of days.
